| 1 | Predictive Learning via Rule Ensembles (RuleFit) | Jerome H. Friedman; Bogdan E. Popescu | 2008 · Annals of Applied Statistics (also arXiv 0811.1679) | Builds decision rules from tree ensembles, then fits a sparse L1 linear model over original features plus those rules. Predictions stay a weighted sum of readable terms (linear features and conjunction rules). | Established that interaction capacity and linear-model interpretability can coexist. Became the standard reference for “rules as basis functions + Lasso.” | Separates rule generation (trees) from rule selection (sparse linear), rather than stopping at a black-box forest or a pure rule list. | Direct ancestor of SymbolicFormula `sparse_linear`: raw + stump + formula ≈ additive RuleFit; rule basis (E5) ≈ RuleFit interactions. Explains UIL’s E5 finding that rules starve under too-strong L1 until the budget is relaxed. |
| 2 | Accurate Intelligible Models with Pairwise Interactions (GA²M) | Yin Lou; Rich Caruana; Johannes Gehrke; Giles Hooker | 2013 · ACM SIGKDD (KDD) | Extends generalized additive models with selected pairwise interaction terms while keeping modular, plottable contributions per term. | Showed pairwise interactions can raise accuracy without abandoning intelligibility; foundation for later EBM / GA²M practice. | Keeps additivity at the term level (including pairs as named units) so explanations remain modular. | Justifies readable interaction capacity (UIL rule basis) as a published, legitimate path—not a retreat from accuracy. |
| 3 | Intelligible Models for HealthCare: Predicting Pneumonia Risk and Hospital 30-day Readmission | Rich Caruana; Yin Lou; Johannes Gehrke; Paul Koch; Marc Sturm; Noemie Elhadad | 2015 · ACM SIGKDD (KDD) | Deploys intelligible GAMs in clinical risk prediction; shows domain experts can inspect and edit harmful learned patterns. | Landmark that intelligibility + editability matter in high-stakes ML; accuracy alone is insufficient when models encode dangerous shortcuts. | Treats human editing of model structure as part of the method, not only post-hoc explanation. | Closest classical precedent for UIL’s HITL / Editable Scientist thesis (edit principles, cells, basis terms). Claim capability now; user benefit still needs a study. |
| 4 | InterpretML: A Unified Framework for Machine Learning Interpretability (includes Explainable Boosting Machine) | Harsha Nori; Samuel Jenkins; Paul Koch; Rich Caruana | 2019 · arXiv:1909.09223 (InterpretML open-source; EBM lineage from Microsoft Research / prior GA²M work) | Glass-box framework featuring EBM: cyclic gradient boosting of shallow trees per feature (and optional pairs), often near RF/XGBoost accuracy while remaining additive and plottable. | Existence proof that “readable at GBDT-ish parity” is repeatedly achievable on tabular data; popularized glass-box vs black-box tooling under one API. | Round-robin cyclic boosting with tiny learning rates so feature order is less arbitrary; pairs remain optional named terms. | Primary peer for SymbolicFormula. Cite EBM as parity precedent; position UIL as composed symbolic basis + sparse readout with VersionStore provenance—not as inventing glass-box tabular learning. Motivates optional EBM-style cyclic fit (Gate 2: small gain; default OFF). |
| 5 | XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System | Tianqi Chen; Carlos Guestrin | 2016 · ACM SIGKDD (KDD) | Highly optimized gradient-boosted trees with regularization, sparsity handling, and system design for scale; became the default strong tabular baseline. | Set the practical accuracy bar for medium tabular problems in competitions and industry. | Combined algorithmic GBDT advances with production-grade systems engineering. | Primary black-box accuracy baseline. UIL’s defendable claim is near-parity under readability, not beating XGBoost everywhere. Also an adapter in UIL’s classifier registry. |
| 6 | Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on typical tabular data? | Leo Grinsztajn; Edouard Oyallon; Gaël Varoquaux | 2022 · NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks | Large benchmark (~45 datasets, heavy HPO): tuned tree ensembles outperform deep tabular models on medium-sized data; analyzes inductive biases (irregular targets, uninformative features, non-rotation-invariant features). | Reset expectations: residual headroom above tuned GBDT on ordinary tabular is small; “more architecture” often fails to buy accuracy. | Careful dataset selection + shared HPO compute so comparisons are not anecdote-driven. | Explains why cognitive-shell accuracy Δ ≈ 0 (or slightly negative when hard-wired) is literature-consistent. Supports changing the competition axis to readability / OOD / editability. |
| 7 | Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model (TabPFN v2 / related TabPFN line) | Noah Hollmann and collaborators (Prior Labs / TabPFN team) | 2025 · Nature (v2); earlier TabPFN versions 2022–2023 | Prior-data fitted network approximating Bayesian inference over a synthetic prior of tabular tasks; strong on small-to-medium n with little tuning. | Showed foundation-model style priors can dominate small tabular regimes without classical HPO. | In-context / prior-fitted approach rather than per-dataset deep training from scratch. | Correct role: ceiling probe / optional small-n router strategy (`D3_tiny_tabpfn`)—never the default Editable Scientist learner (not glass-box). |
| 8 | AI Feynman: A Physics-Inspired Method for Symbolic Regression | Silviu-Marian Udrescu; Max Tegmark | 2020 · Science Advances | Recursive symbolic regression combining neural fits with physics-inspired reductions (symmetry, separability, etc.); recovers Feynman-lecture equations at high rates vs prior tools. | Modern landmark that symbolic methods can rediscover real scientific formulas; released a large SR benchmark database. | Physics inductive biases + problem decomposition, not pure genetic search alone. | Anchors UIL’s M4 “scientist” / symbolic OOD claim. Paper angle: frozen symbolic suite—not “formulas raise breast_cancer accuracy.” |
| 9 | AI Feynman 2.0: Pareto-Optimal Symbolic Regression Exploiting Graph Modularity | Silviu-Marian Udrescu; Andrew Tan; Jiahai Feng; Orisvaldo Neto; Tailin Wu; Max Tegmark | 2020 · NeurIPS | Improves robustness and discovery via Pareto complexity–accuracy frontiers, generalized modularity from NN gradients, and related machinery. | Raised the bar for noisy/harder symbolic discovery and for reporting accuracy–complexity tradeoffs. | Explicit Pareto frontier + broader modularity discovery than AI Feynman 1.0. | Design pattern for UIL symbolic benchmarking (complexity vs fit; OOD stress), not for IID tabular leaderboards. |
| 10 | DreamCoder: Growing generalizable, interpretable knowledge with wake–sleep Bayesian program learning | Kevin Ellis; Catherine Wong; Maxwell Nye; Mathias Sablé-Meyer; Lucas Morales; Luke Hewitt; Luc Cary; Armando Solar-Lezama; Joshua B. Tenenbaum (core team) | 2021 onward · PLDI / Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society line | Wake–sleep program learning that grows a library of abstractions; candidates enter only if they improve a compression / utility objective (MDL-style “pay for themselves”). | Named the failure mode of unbounded library growth without utility gating; showed libraries stay useful when admission is selective. | Utility-gated abstraction learning as the core loop, not event-triggered mutation alone. | Direct research framing for utility-gated formula admission + newborn L1 sub-budget. Implemented in UIL; Gate 2 failed as default—still the right diagnosis for “library grows, accuracy unchanged.” |
| 11 | Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KAN) | Ziming Liu and collaborators | 2024+ · arXiv / expanding follow-up venues | Networks with learnable univariate functions on edges; often sparsified and promoted for symbolic extraction / plottable univariate terms. | Reopened interest in spline/univariate bases as readable alternatives to opaque MLP hidden units. | Places learnable univariate maps on edges (KAN formulation) rather than only fixed activations on nodes. | Inspiration for opt-in `use_kan_basis` (truncated-power terms). Gate 2 did not support default ON—keep as race/opt-in axis. |
| 12 | Efficient and Robust Automated Machine Learning (Auto-sklearn) | Matthias Feurer; Aaron Klein; Katharina Eggensperger; Jost Springenberg; Manuel Blum; Frank Hutter | 2015 · NeurIPS | CASH system over sklearn pipelines with Bayesian optimization, meta-learning warm-starts from many prior datasets, and ensemble construction. | Showed meta-learning helps AutoML when experience spans many datasets; won ChaLearn AutoML phases. | Combined BO + meta-feature warm-start + ensemble selection in one robust system. | Sample-complexity lesson: transferable principles need OpenML-scale experience, not ~16 journal rows on one isolated `state_dir` run. Kinship for fingerprint→strategy routing (same family, thinner than full Auto-sklearn). |
| 13 | Auto-Sklearn 2.0: Hands-free AutoML via Meta-Learning | Matthias Feurer; Katharina Eggensperger; Stefan Falkner; Marius Lindauer; Frank Hutter | 2022 · Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR) | Meta-learned portfolios, successive-halving budgets, and meta-selection over AutoML itself for hands-free operation under time limits. | Push toward automating AutoML design choices; strong results under rigid budgets. | Treats the AutoML system configuration as itself a meta-learning problem. | Design kinship for Architecture Race (successive halving) + domain routing; reminder that routing must be validated held-out across tasks (still open in UIL brief). |
| 14 | Particle Swarm Optimization | James Kennedy; Russell Eberhart | 1995 · IEEE International Conference on Neural Networks (ICNN) | Population-based optimizer where particles share best-known positions to search continuous spaces. | Foundational swarm metaheuristic used widely in engineering and ML hyperparameter / feature search. | Social sharing of best positions rather than genetic crossover alone. | Classic UIL search path (PSO / principle-guided). Also grounds the honesty that a separable linear surrogate makes a large swarm re-derive an argsort—complexity pathology, not “PSO failed.” |
| 15 | Hyperband: A Novel Bandit-Based Approach to Hyperparameter Optimization | Lisha Li; Kevin Jamieson; Giulia DeSalvo; Afshin Rostamizadeh; Ameet Talwalkar | 2018 · Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR) | Extends Successive Halving into brackets trading number of configs vs resources per config; discards weak arms early. | Standard efficient HPO under finite budgets; widely adopted in AutoML stacks. | Outer loop over Successive Halving brackets to solve the n vs B/n tradeoff. | Blueprint for Architecture Race rungs. Also predicts winner’s curse if final selection uses a noisy single validation split (seen in UIL Gate 2). |
| 16 | Population Based Training of Neural Networks | Max Jaderberg; Valentin Dalibard; Simon Osindero; Wojciech M. Czarnecki; Jeff Donahue; Ali Razavi; Oriol Vinyals; Tim Green; Iain Dunning; Karen Simonyan; Chrisantha Fernando; Koray Kavukcuoglu | 2017 · arXiv:1711.09846 (DeepMind) | Trains a population in parallel; periodically copies winners (exploit) and perturbs hyperparameters (explore), learning schedules online. | Showed online explore/exploit can find hyperparameter schedules, not only fixed configs, at roughly population-training cost. | Couples weight inheritance with continuous hyperparameter perturbation during training. | Motivates architecture variation in the default path so journals contain learnable experience (classic mode otherwise keeps architecture knobs nearly constant). |
| 17 | An Efficient Approach for Assessing Hyperparameter Importance (fANOVA) | Frank Hutter; Holger Hoos; Kevin Leyton-Brown | 2014 · ICML | Fits a random-forest surrogate over configuration→performance and decomposes variance to rank hyperparameter importance and interactions. | Standard tool to explain which knobs matter after HPO / AutoML logs. | Functional ANOVA on an empirical performance surface from logged configs. | Natural analysis over UIL journals once architecture knobs vary; can supply provenance for which axes deserve candidates or mined rules. |
| 18 | On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation | Gavin C. Cawley; Nicola L. C. Talbot | 2010 · Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR) | Shows variance of the model-selection criterion causes overfitting comparable in size to algorithm differences; warns against biased evaluation when selection and test leak. | Made nested / careful evaluation part of best practice; named selection overfitting as first-class. | Focuses on overfitting the selection score, not only the training loss. | Explains Architecture Race regressions on small n and vanishing “cognition wins” when folds increase. Directly motivates multi-split race judging (`_extra_val_splits`) and Gate 2 culture. |
| 19 | The reusable holdout: Preserving validity in adaptive data analysis | Cynthia Dwork; Vitaly Feldman; Moritz Hardt; Toniann Pitassi; Omer Reingold; Aaron Roth | 2015 · Science | Shows adaptive reuse of a holdout invalidates classical guarantees; proposes Thresholdout (DP-inspired noise gate) so holdout answers remain valid under many queries. | Brought differential-privacy ideas into adaptive scientific data analysis; named a concrete reusable-holdout mechanism. | Validity under adaptivity, not only fixed one-shot test sets. | Grounds `cognition/thresholdout.py` for champion bars and standing A/B gates—needed because UIL repeatedly queries the same evaluation culture. |
| 20 | Improving Reproducibility in Machine Learning Research (A Report from the NeurIPS 2019 Reproducibility Program) | Joelle Pineau; Philippe Vincent-Lamarre; Koustuv Sinha; Vincent Larivière; Alina Beygelzimer; Florence d’Alché-Buc; Emily Fox; Hugo Larochelle (and program contributors) | 2021 · Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR) | Documents reproducibility failures and promotes checklists: seeds, multiple runs, clear eval protocols, code release norms. | Cultural shift: single-run claims are insufficient; reporting standards matter as much as algorithms. | Community-process paper with concrete checklist artifacts adopted by venues. | Supports cross-seed `eval_crossseed`, identity-control existence audits, and explicit seeding (e.g. liblinear `random_state` fix). |
| 21 | The Bitter Lesson | Richard S. Sutton | 2019 · Essay (incompleteideas.net; widely cited in ML discourse) | Argues that general methods leveraging computation repeatedly beat approaches that inject human domain knowledge into the agent. | Framing device for why elaborate reasoning machinery often loses on raw performance metrics. | Concise historical thesis across AI decades (chess, speech, vision, Go). | Frames cognitive-shell IID-accuracy wash as field-expected; still allows specialized metrics (readability, OOD symbolic, editability) where “more FLOPs on black boxes” is not the goal. |
| 22 | Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference (and related do-calculus papers) | Judea Pearl | Book 2000 / 2nd ed. 2009 · Cambridge University Press; do-calculus papers mid-1990s onward | Formal distinction between observing and intervening; do-calculus for identifying causal effects from assumptions + data. | Made causal claims scientifically checkable beyond correlation; standard language for interventions. | Graphical models + do-operator unifying structural and interventional reasoning. | Epistemology for the causal intervention engine: control/treatment trials and majority-of-3 before `CONFIRMED_CAUSAL`; correlational mining alone is not enough. |
| 23 | Counterfactual Risk Minimization / Batch Learning from Logged Bandit Feedback | Adith Swaminathan; Thorsten Joachims | 2015 · ICML / JMLR line | Learning from logged interaction data requires correcting for the logging policy (propensity); naive ERM on logs is biased. | Standard warning for off-policy learning from search/bandit logs; CRM estimators. | Focused estimators for batch logged bandit feedback rather than only online RL. | Journal mining inherits PSO’s logging policy. Explains propensity footnotes on principles (F6); full CRM reweighting remains future work. |
| 24 | Classifier Systems / XCS (accuracy-based Learning Classifier System) | John H. Holland (LCS foundations); Stewart W. Wilson (XCS) | Holland 1970s–80s; Wilson XCS mid-1990s onward (Evolutionary Computation / related venues) | Evolves a population of condition→action rules with fitness from experience; XCS emphasizes accuracy-based fitness and generalization. | Long lineage of rule-based adaptive systems combining RL credit assignment with genetic search over rules. | Online rule population with genetic discovery, distinct from batch DT mining. | Architectural ancestry for the principle library (conditions → overrides, confidence, tiers). Cite as kinship—not algorithmic identity (UIL mines regret journals with DTs; XCS is typically online RL). |
| 25 | Power to the People: The Role of Humans in Interactive Machine Learning (and related Amershi IML work) | Saleema Amershi; Maya Cakmak; William Bradley Knox; Todd Kulesza (representative IML survey/essay line) | 2014 · AI Magazine (and CHI/IUI interactive-ML literature) | Frames ML systems designed for tight human feedback loops—correction, steering, and rapid iteration—not only batch train/test. | Normalized HITL as a research area with UX + learning metrics, not only accuracy. | Centers human interaction design alongside algorithms. | Framing for VersionStore edit/reject/replay. Software path exists; human benefit unproven—needs the HITL pilot (brief priority 1). |
| 26 | Regression Shrinkage and Selection via the Lasso | Robert Tibshirani | 1996 · Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B | L1-penalized regression that simultaneously shrinks and selects variables, producing sparse linear models. | Foundation of modern sparse linear modeling and basis-term selection. | Convex L1 penalty yielding exact zeros (selection) with shrinkage. | Mathematical core of SymbolicFormula `sparse_linear` term selection over the composed basis. |
| 27 | Generalized Additive Models | Trevor Hastie; Robert Tibshirani | 1986 paper / 1990 monograph · Statistical Science / Chapman & Hall | Models of the form g(E[y]) = β0 + Σ fj(xj): flexible per-feature shapes with additive structure for interpretability. | Classical statistical basis for “intelligible nonlinear” modeling later revived by GA²M/EBM. | Separates smoothness per feature while forbidding opaque cross-feature entanglement (until later GA²M extensions). | Conceptual root of additive readable tabular models; UIL’s additive basis sum sits in this tradition. |
| 28 | Distilling Free-Form Natural Laws from Experimental Data | Michael Schmidt; Hod Lipson | 2009 · Science | Evolutionary symbolic regression discovering free-form natural laws from motion-capture / experimental data (Eureqa lineage). | High-profile demonstration that SR can recover scientific invariants from data. | Emphasis on invariant / law discovery from physical experimental streams. | Historical SR precedent behind AI Feynman and UIL’s scientist/OOD narrative. |
| 29 | A Unified Approach to Interpreting Model Predictions (SHAP) | Scott M. Lundberg; Su-In Lee | 2017 · NeurIPS | Unified additive feature-attribution framework (Shapley values) for explaining predictions of black-box models. | De facto standard for post-hoc explanation tooling. | Game-theoretic unification of several attribution methods. | Contrast paper: related work should say UIL is inherent glass-box + editable knowledge, not “XGBoost + SHAP.” |
